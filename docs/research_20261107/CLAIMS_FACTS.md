# Claims facts summary (researched 2026-11-07)

Full data with source URLs and exact quotes is in `claims_facts.json` (52 facts plus an `unverified` list).

## US DOT refund rule (14 CFR 260, eCFR current to 2026-11-05)
- A significant change triggers a refund. Delay thresholds are 3h+ for domestic and 6h+ for international, measured from either departure or arrival. A change of airport, extra connections or a downgrade also counts.
- Refunds are due within 7 business days for credit cards and 20 calendar days for other payment types. Airlines cannot impose a voucher unless the passenger affirmatively agrees.
- The bag fee is refunded if the bag arrives more than 12h late (domestic), 15h late (international, nonstop leg of 12h or less) or 30h late (international, nonstop leg over 12h). A Mishandled Baggage Report is required.
- The rule took effect 2024-06-25, and airlines had until 2024-10-28 to comply. Part 260 has not been amended since 2024-08-12.
- Changes since 2025:
  - DOT paused enforcement only for flights that are renumbered but operate with no significant change. The pause began 2025-12-05 and was extended to 2027-07-07, while DOT works on a rule that may change the "cancelled flight" definition.
  - DOT withdrew its delay-compensation ANPRM on 2025-11-17.
- **Not verified:** DOT complaint portal, mailing address and phone. transportation.gov blocked fetches.

## EU261 (Your Europe, last checked 21/09/2026)
- Compensation is EUR 250 / 400 / 600 by distance band. A EUR 300 tier applies for long-haul flights delayed 3–4h.
- Compensation is due for arrivals 3h+ late unless the cause was extraordinary circumstances. Your Europe gives no court basis. The basis is the CJEU Sturgeon/Böck judgment (C-402/07 and C-432/07, 19 Nov 2009), cited in the 2026 amending regulation.
- Care covers food, refreshments, a hotel if needed, transport and 2 calls. A departure delay of 5h+ gives the right to a ticket refund.
- To complain, go to the airline first. If there is no reply within 2 months, go to the national enforcement body (Commission list PDF, 2024-01-30).
- Reform:
  - Political agreement on 2026-06-15. Parliament approved on 2026-07-07 (third reading), and the act was signed on 2026-09-16.
  - It applies 12 months after entry into force, so the current rules still apply.
  - The amounts and the 3h threshold stay the same. The EUR 600 band can be halved when the arrival delay is 4h or less.
  - The OJ number and publication date are not verified.

## UK261 (CAA)
- GBP 220 / 350. For flights over 3,500 km, GBP 260 for a 3–4h delay and GBP 520 for more than 4h.
- To escalate, go to the airline first. If unresolved, or after 8 weeks, go to an ADR scheme (AviationADR or CEDR). If the airline is not in a scheme, CAA PACT may help, but its decisions are not binding.

## Canada APPR (SOR/2019-150, current to 2026-10-19, last amended 2022-09-08)
- Large carriers pay CAD 400 / 700 / 1,000 for delays of 3–6h, 6–9h and 9h+. Small carriers pay CAD 125 / 250 / 500.
- A passenger who takes a refund instead gets CAD 400 (large) or CAD 125 (small).
- Claim within 1 year. The airline has 30 days to respond. After writing to the airline and waiting 30 days, file a CTA complaint.

## Montreal Convention
- The baggage limit is **1,519 SDR**, effective 2024-12-28, up from 1,288 SDR. Confirmed by a CTA notice and UK SI 2025/123.
- Written complaint deadlines are 7 days for damage and 21 days for delay (Your Europe). Court action has a 2-year limit (CTA).

## US card disputes (CFPB / Reg Z 1026.13)
- Send a written billing-error notice within 60 days of the first statement showing the charge. Services "not delivered as agreed" count as a billing error.
- The issuer must acknowledge within 30 days and resolve within 2 billing cycles (90 days at most).
- Visa/Mastercard rules are deliberately excluded.

## EU Package Travel Directive (2015/2302)
- The organiser is liable for every service in the package and must fix problems at no cost to the traveller. Where services fail, the traveller is entitled to a price reduction, compensation, or both.
- If the organiser cancels, the traveller gets a refund plus compensation where appropriate. If the cause is unavoidable or extraordinary, the refund is full but there is no extra compensation.
- A price rise of more than 8% lets the traveller cancel without a fee. Insolvency protection covers refunds and repatriation.
- The amending directive was signed 2026-04-29. It applies 34 months after entry into force, so the current rules still apply. Its number (2026/1024) and OJ date are not verified.
