# Research: Booking.com, Airbnb and hotel message scams: how to tell a fake payment request

Compiled 2026-11-07 for RuinMyTrip.com. Official platform and government sources only. Quotes are 25 words or fewer.

Verification: quotes from Booking.com, Airbnb, Vrbo, NCSC, Scamwatch and CAFC pages were checked as exact substrings of the downloaded page text. FTC quotes came from WebFetch extraction because consumer.ftc.gov blocks direct download; these are marked [FTC-extract].

Counts: 1=13, 2=16, 3=17, 4=15

## 1. What the platforms say they will never ask

- **Booking.com**: Booking.com says it will never ask travellers to share credit card details by email, phone, text or WhatsApp.  
  > "We'll never ask you to share credit card details by email, over the phone, through text or WhatsApp."  
  Source: [Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/trust_and_safety/travellers.html)
- **Booking.com**: Booking.com says it will never ask for a bank transfer that differs from the payment policy in the booking confirmation.  
  > "We'll never ask you to make a bank transfer that is different from the payment policy details in your booking confirmation."  
  Source: [Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/trust_and_safety/travellers.html)
- **Booking.com**: No legitimate payment or reservation change requires gift cards, or card details by phone, text or email.  
  > "No legitimate transaction (for example, payments or reservation changes) will ever need you to pay specifically with gift cards."  
  Source: [Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/trust_and_safety/travellers.html)
- **Booking.com**: Customer Service only asks for reservation ID/PIN, never the account password or card number.  
  > "Our Customer Service representatives will only ask you to share your reservation ID and/or reservation PIN."  
  Source: [Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/trust_and_safety/travellers.html)
- **Booking.com**: Booking.com treats any request to pay outside its official website or app as a red flag.  
  > "If asked to pay outside of the official website or app, treat it as a red flag."  
  Source: [Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/articles/avoid-hotel-scams.html)
- **Booking.com**: Booking.com says reputable platforms never ask for double (duplicate) payments.  
  > "Reputable platforms never ask for double payments."  
  Source: [Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/articles/avoid-hotel-scams.html)
- **Booking.com**: After confirmation, no extra online payments should be needed except those made at the property; avoid bank transfer, prepaid card or crypto requests.  
  > "Avoid requests for bank transfers, prepaid cards, or cryptocurrency. Once your booking is confirmed, you shouldn’t incur any additional online payments"  
  Source: [Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/articles/avoid-hotel-scams.html)
- **Airbnb**: It is against Airbnb policy for hosts to ask guests to take the booking off Airbnb or communicate off-platform (e.g. WhatsApp) before booking.  
  > "for hosts to ask you to take your booking off of Airbnb or to communicate outside the Airbnb platform through a messaging service (ex: WhatsApp"  
  Source: [Airbnb](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/209)
- **Airbnb**: Paying for a booking outside Airbnb loses AirCover protection.  
  > "If you choose to pay for your booking outside of Airbnb, you will lose the"  
  Source: [Airbnb](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/209)
- **Airbnb**: Users may not ask or encourage others to pay outside Airbnb; payments for bookings and mandatory fees must stay on platform (limited exceptions for disclosed fees/hotel deposits).  
  > "may not ask or encourage another user to send or receive payments outside of Airbnb."  
  Source: [Airbnb](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2799)  
  Note: Exceptions: some software-connected hosts and hotels may collect disclosed mandatory fees/deposits off-platform; must appear in price breakdown before booking.
- **Airbnb**: Airbnb will never call to ask you to change payout method or share one-time codes, passwords, or payment details.  
  > "We will never call to ask you to change your payout method or share one-time codes, account passwords, or payment or payout details."  
  Source: [Airbnb](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/971)
- **Airbnb**: Payment requests from anyone claiming to represent Airbnb that ask you to pay outside Airbnb are a fraud sign to report.  
  > "An unfamiliar request to pay outside Airbnb, including payment requests from anyone claiming to represent Airbnb."  
  Source: [Airbnb](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2799)
- **Vrbo**: Vrbo says it will never ask you to make a payment outside its secure system; fake emails asking for transfer/card payments should be reported.  
  > "We will never ask you to make a payment outside of our secure system."  
  Source: [Vrbo (Expedia Group)](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/What-Smishing-and-Vishing)

## 2. Typical scam pattern (official descriptions)

- **Booking.com**: Phishing messages usually claim to come from the property (sometimes Booking.com) and include convincing stay details.  
  > "Stay alert for messages that most often claim to come from the accommodation or sometimes even"  
  Source: [Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/trust_and_safety/travellers.html)  
  Note: Text continues: "Booking.com, and include convincing details about the dates and cost of your stay."
- **Booking.com**: These messages typically urgently demand that you (re)share payment details to keep the booking.  
  > "Phishing messages also typically come with urgent requests to (re)share payment information in order to retain your booking."  
  Source: [Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/trust_and_safety/travellers.html)
- **Booking.com**: Compromised traveller data can be used for phishing via WhatsApp, phone or email.  
  > "compromise of traveler data may be used for fraud such as phishing, through channels like WhatsApp, but also phone or email."  
  Source: [Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/trust_and_safety/travellers.html)
- **Booking.com**: Example Booking.com gives of an urgency/cancellation threat to watch for.  
  > "transfer the money to a bank account within 24 hours or the booking will be canceled"  
  Source: [Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/trust_and_safety/travellers.html)
- **Booking.com**: Partner (hotel) account compromise, often via phishing, gives criminals Extranet access, fraudulent transactions and guest details.  
  > "Frequently a result of phishing, this involves unauthorized and illegal access to your Extranet account."  
  Source: [Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/trust_and_safety/partners.html)
- **Booking.com**: Partner account compromise can result in fraudulent transactions and unauthorized access to guest details.  
  > "It can result in fraudulent transactions and unauthorized access to guest details."  
  Source: [Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/trust_and_safety/partners.html)
- **Booking.com**: Scammers pose as a hotel or platform, claim a failed payment or incomplete booking, and request duplicate payment.  
  > "Sometimes scammers pretend to be from a hotel or booking platform and claim a payment has failed or your booking is incomplete."  
  Source: [Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/articles/avoid-hotel-scams.html)
- **Airbnb**: Fraudulent messages use urgency and threats such as loss of a reservation if you do not click or provide info immediately.  
  > "often have an urgent tone and threaten consequences, like account suspension, loss of a reservation or booking"  
  Source: [Airbnb](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/971)
- **Airbnb**: Fake emails copy real Airbnb text (reservation confirmations, reminders) and use the Airbnb logo and a fake From address.  
  > "Fraudulent emails often include the Airbnb logo and a fake Airbnb address in the "From" line."  
  Source: [Airbnb](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/971)
- **Airbnb**: Fraudulent messages can link to fake Airbnb pages built to steal information.  
  > "Fraudulent emails and messages can contain links to fake Airbnb web pages that try to steal your information."  
  Source: [Airbnb](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/971)
- **Vrbo**: If a host email account is compromised, criminals can intercept guest inquiries and request booking payments.  
  > "If your email account is compromised, criminals can intercept inquiries from guests and request booking payments without your knowledge."  
  Source: [Vrbo (Expedia Group)](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/What-Smishing-and-Vishing)
- **Vrbo**: Smishing arrives by SMS or WhatsApp, pressuring immediate action and linking to a fake website.  
  > "Victims of smishing receive an SMS or WhatsApp message pressuring them to take immediate action."  
  Source: [Vrbo (Expedia Group)](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/What-Smishing-and-Vishing)
- **Scamwatch (ACCC)**: Scammers build convincing fake versions of booking sites to harvest personal and banking details (2016 alert).  
  > "scammers create very convincing fake versions of the site which they can use to collect personal details and banking information."  
  Source: [Scamwatch (ACCC, Australia)](https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/about-us/news-and-alerts/dont-book-a-holiday-with-a-scammer)  
  Note: Alert dated 5 Dec 2016; older but official.
- **Scamwatch (ACCC)**: Scammers send official-looking fake confirmation emails imitating logos/addresses (2016 alert).  
  > "the scammers may send ‘confirmation’ emails which appear to be official, using a logo or email address that imitates the real thing."  
  Source: [Scamwatch (ACCC, Australia)](https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/about-us/news-and-alerts/dont-book-a-holiday-with-a-scammer)
- **US FTC**: Scammers send phishing emails that look like they come from a recognisable airline or hotel.  
  > "scammers send phishing emails with travel offers that look like they’re from an airline or hotel you recognize"  
  Source: [US Federal Trade Commission](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2025/06/how-avoid-travel-website-scams) [FTC-extract]  
  Note: Quote via WebFetch extraction (consumer.ftc.gov blocks direct download); alert dated June 20, 2025.
- **US FTC**: Money paid by the methods scammers insist on is almost impossible to recover.  
  > "once they’ve collected the money, it’s almost impossible to get it back."  
  Source: [US Federal Trade Commission](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/avoid-scams-when-you-travel) [FTC-extract]  
  Note: Quote via WebFetch extraction.

## 3. If you clicked or paid

- **Booking.com**: If you clicked a link or shared info, contact Booking.com Customer Service immediately and report it; it goes to the Security team.  
  > "Report that you may have been scammed and we will ensure this information reaches our dedicated Security team for further investigation."  
  Source: [Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/trust_and_safety/travellers.html)
- **Booking.com**: Reset your Booking.com password and enable two-factor authentication.  
  > "Reset your password to your Booking.com account and make sure to enable two-factor authentication."  
  Source: [Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/trust_and_safety/travellers.html)
- **Booking.com**: Contact your bank/payment provider, ask to block cards and reissue, and watch for unrecognised charges.  
  > "Reach out to your bank and/or payment provider, inform them of your concerns and ask them to block your current card(s) and issue new ones."  
  Source: [Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/trust_and_safety/travellers.html)
- **Booking.com**: Contacting the bank quickly might help stop or recover payments.  
  > "Contacting them quickly might help you stop or recover payments."  
  Source: [Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/articles/avoid-hotel-scams.html)
- **Booking.com**: For larger sums or sensitive info, inform the police.  
  > "For larger sums or sensitive information, informing the police provides an extra layer of protection and support."  
  Source: [Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/articles/avoid-hotel-scams.html)
- **Airbnb**: If you interacted with a suspicious communication, contact Airbnb directly, and your bank if payment info was shared.  
  > "If you’ve shared any payment information, you should also contact your bank or other financial institution to let them know."  
  Source: [Airbnb](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/971)
- **Airbnb**: Report fake Airbnb websites at airbnb.com/report-phishing (does not open a support case).  
  > "let us know by reporting the website’s URL at"  
  Source: [Airbnb](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/971)  
  Note: URL given: https://www.airbnb.com/report-phishing
- **Airbnb**: Report suspicious messages by contacting Airbnb or flagging the message in Messages ("Report this message").  
  > "If you get a suspicious message, let us know by"  
  Source: [Airbnb](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3017)
- **Vrbo**: Vrbo asks users to report scam attacks via Contact Us; support numbers in US 877-202-9331, outside US +1-512-782-0805.  
  > "To report any kind of scam attacks, select"  
  Source: [Vrbo (Expedia Group)](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/What-Smishing-and-Vishing)  
  Note: Phone numbers from https://help.vrbo.com/articles/About-offline-booking (stated in a different context: false warnings).
- **US FTC**: Paid by credit card: report to the card issuer immediately and ask for a refund.  
  > "Report it to the credit card issuer immediately."  
  Source: [US Federal Trade Commission](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-do-if-you-were-scammed) [FTC-extract]  
  Note: Quote via WebFetch extraction.
- **US FTC**: Paid by bank transfer: report to your bank immediately and ask them to reverse the payment.  
  > "Ask them to reverse the payment and refund your money."  
  Source: [US Federal Trade Commission](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-do-if-you-were-scammed) [FTC-extract]  
  Note: Quote via WebFetch extraction.
- **US FTC**: Report scammers to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov (official US reporting URL: https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/).  
  > "Report a scammer to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov."  
  Source: [US Federal Trade Commission](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-do-if-you-were-scammed) [FTC-extract]  
  Note: Quote via WebFetch extraction; reportfraud.ftc.gov returned HTTP 200 on 2026-11-07.
- **UK NCSC**: Victims in England, Wales or NI should report to Report Fraud (reportfraud.police.uk, 0300 123 2040); Scotland: Police Scotland 101.  
  > "you should report this to Report Fraud at"  
  Source: [UK National Cyber Security Centre](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/phishing-scams/report-scam-email)  
  Note: NCSC now names "Report Fraud" (https://www.reportfraud.police.uk/) as the reporting service, not Action Fraud.
- **UK NCSC**: Forward suspicious emails to report@phishing.gov.uk (NCSC Suspicious Email Reporting Service).  
  > "If you have received an email which you’re not quite sure about, forward it to"  
  Source: [UK National Cyber Security Centre](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/phishing-scams/report-scam-email)  
  Note: Address (Cloudflare-obfuscated on page, decoded): report@phishing.gov.uk
- **UK NCSC**: Forward suspicious texts to 7726 free of charge.  
  > "allows customers to report suspicious text messages for free by forwarding it to"  
  Source: [UK National Cyber Security Centre](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/phishing-scams/report-scam-text-message)
- **Scamwatch (ACCC)**: If personal or financial info was stolen, contact your bank or card provider now to stop transactions; report at scamwatch.gov.au/report-a-scam.  
  > "Contact your bank or credit card provider now and tell them to stop any transactions."  
  Source: [Scamwatch (ACCC, Australia)](https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/report-a-scam)
- **Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre**: Report fraud to local police and the CAFC; online via reportcyberandfraud.canada.ca; phone 1-888-495-8501 (page notes the line was currently unavailable).  
  > "it is essential that those who experience, or fall victim, report it to local police and the CAFC."  
  Source: [Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre](https://antifraudcentre-centreantifraude.ca/report-signalez-eng.htm)  
  Note: Online reporting: https://reportcyberandfraud.canada.ca/ ; page states toll-free line currently unavailable.

## 4. How to check a payment request is real

- **Booking.com**: Check the details against your booking confirmation before clicking any link in a message that appears to come from the property or Booking.com.  
  > "be sure to double-check the details in your booking confirmation before clicking on any links."  
  Source: [Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/trust_and_safety/travellers.html)
- **Booking.com**: If in any doubt, contact Booking.com Customer Service before taking action.  
  > "If in any doubt and before taking any action, contact our Customer Service team."  
  Source: [Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/trust_and_safety/travellers.html)
- **Booking.com**: Payment and deposit amounts are in the confirmation email; urgent payment demands or pressure to talk off-platform should go to Customer Service.  
  > "Find the correct info on prepayments, damage deposits, and more in your Booking.com confirmation email."  
  Source: [Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/trust_and_safety/travellers.html)
- **Booking.com**: Check reservation status directly in the official website or app.  
  > "If in doubt, you can easily check your reservation status directly on the official website or app."  
  Source: [Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/articles/avoid-hotel-scams.html)
- **Booking.com**: Verify the full sender address, not just display name; official emails come from Booking.com domain.  
  > "carefully verify the entire email address, not just the displayed name."  
  Source: [Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/articles/avoid-hotel-scams.html)
- **Booking.com**: Hover over links first; never pay via links in messages claiming to be from the platform or property.  
  > "Never click on links or make payments requested by an email or message claiming to be from our platform or the property"  
  Source: [Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/articles/avoid-hotel-scams.html)
- **Booking.com**: Use contact details from the official site, not from the suspicious message.  
  > "Instead of using contact details given in a suspicious message, find them directly on our official site."  
  Source: [Booking.com](https://www.booking.com/articles/avoid-hotel-scams.html)
- **Airbnb**: A real Airbnb link starts with https://www.airbnb.com or a country domain (e.g. airbnb.co.uk); otherwise close it.  
  > "A real link to Airbnb will begin with"  
  Source: [Airbnb](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/971)
- **Airbnb**: Legitimate Airbnb emails only come from listed domains (e.g. @airbnb.com, @guest.airbnb.com); lookalikes like @bnb.com are fake.  
  > "If it's not sent from one of these addresses, it’s not from Airbnb."  
  Source: [Airbnb](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/971)
- **Airbnb**: To check, do not click received links; navigate to Airbnb yourself or use the app.  
  > "do not click on any links you’ve received – instead, separately navigate to the Airbnb website from your browser or use the app"  
  Source: [Airbnb](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/971)
- **Airbnb**: Mandatory charges must appear in the listing price breakdown at checkout; optional extras go through the Resolution Center.  
  > "all mandatory charges must be included in the listing’s price breakdown at checkout."  
  Source: [Airbnb](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/209)
- **Airbnb**: Paying by wire or bank transfer may indicate a fraudulent reservation.  
  > "If you pay via wire or bank transfer it may be a fraudulent reservation."  
  Source: [Airbnb](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/209)
- **Vrbo**: Check the URL; go to the real website rather than following a link.  
  > "double-check the URL, and go to the actual website’s web address rather than follow a link."  
  Source: [Vrbo (Expedia Group)](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/What-Smishing-and-Vishing)
- **Scamwatch (ACCC)**: Ignore any payment request outside the approved methods stated on the actual site (2016 alert).  
  > "Any request for payment to be made via a method other than through the approved payment methods stated on the actual site, should be ignored."  
  Source: [Scamwatch (ACCC, Australia)](https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/about-us/news-and-alerts/dont-book-a-holiday-with-a-scammer)
- **US FTC**: Check the real company website directly and type the URL yourself rather than clicking.  
  > "But type in the URL yourself; don’t click the link."  
  Source: [US Federal Trade Commission](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2025/06/how-avoid-travel-website-scams) [FTC-extract]  
  Note: Quote via WebFetch extraction.
