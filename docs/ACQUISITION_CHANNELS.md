# Where travelers actually look for each other, ranked

Researched 2026-09-16. Ranked on five things: does the audience fit, do the rules allow a link, can
we link at all, how strong is the traveler intent, and what does it risk.

**Nothing here has been joined or posted to.** Where a rule is stated below it came from a public
page read this session or from a source named beside it; where it could not be read, the row says
so and the rule must be checked by a person before anything goes out.

**Two things that are true of every row.** We never join under a false identity, we never scrape a
member list, and we never send an unsolicited message. And a channel that bans promotion is not a
channel we post a link in and hope: it is a channel we either participate in honestly with the site
in the profile, or skip.

---

## Tier 1: fit the product, allow participation, worth doing first

### 1. Facebook groups, city and event specific

* **Audience fit:** very high. A "Oktoberfest 2026" group is a cohort with a date on it.
* **Rules:** vary per group and are always pinned. Many allow a link with disclosure, more allow it
  in a comment.
* **Can we link:** usually yes.
* **Intent:** high. People post "anyone else going" in these groups unprompted.
* **Risk:** low to the site, real to a personal account that joins ten groups in an evening.
* **Status:** **five posts ready** in `docs/ACQUISITION_FACEBOOK.md`. Blocked on a personal account.

### 2. Facebook groups, travel companion and solo travel

* **Audience fit:** the highest of anything on this list, because finding people is the group's
  stated purpose rather than something it tolerates.
* **Rules:** companion groups usually permit a link. Solo travel groups usually do not, and expect it
  in a comment.
* **Intent:** very high.
* **Risk:** low if the account is a real member first.
* **Status:** F2 and F5 written and ready.

### 3. Travellerspoint, the Travel Companions forum

* **Audience fit:** high, and it is one of the last general travel forums still alive. Lonely
  Planet's Thorn Tree, which was the biggest of these, was deleted permanently in September 2021
  ([phocuswire](https://www.phocuswire.com/Lonely-Planet-axes-legendary-Thorn-Tree-travel-community)),
  so what remains is thinner than people assume.
* **Rules, read this session:** **promotional posts are deleted without warning.** A post that
  genuinely answers a question may stand even if it mentions a site, and **a website in the profile
  is explicitly allowed** ([travellerspoint rules discussion](https://www.travellerspoint.com/forum.cfm?thread=128616),
  [FAQ](https://www.travellerspoint.com/faq.cfm)).
* **Can we link:** in the profile yes, in a post only as part of a real answer.
* **Intent:** high, it has a dedicated Travel Companions board.
* **Risk:** low if the rule is respected, immediate removal if it is not.
* **How to use it:** answer three real questions with no link at all, put the site in the profile,
  and only then post publication 9 style copy in a thread where it is the actual answer.

## Tier 2: good fit, stricter or smaller

### 4. Travel Discord servers

* **Examples found this session:** Digital Nomads, Rustic Wanderer Squad for solo travelers, an
  Interrail and Eurail backpacker server, Worldwide Travelers
  ([discord.me travel](https://discord.me/servers/category/travel), [whop roundup](https://whop.com/blog/best-travel-communities/)).
* **Rules:** **not readable without joining**, which is the honest state of this row. Most servers
  have a `#self-promo` channel and ban links everywhere else.
* **Audience fit:** high, especially the solo and Interrail servers.
* **Intent:** medium to high, and conversational rather than search driven.
* **Risk:** low, but a new account dropping a link is removed instantly.
* **How to use it:** join one, read `#rules`, talk for a week, use `#self-promo` only if it exists.

### 5. Meetup and event communities in the cohort cities

* **Audience fit:** high and local rather than traveling, which is a different and useful audience:
  a local who will meet visitors is exactly what the travelers hub is for.
* **Rules:** Meetup itself is for organising events, not posting links, so this means attending or
  hosting rather than posting.
* **Risk:** low. **Cost:** time, and it does not scale, which is fine for the first hundred people.

### 6. Conference and festival attendee groups

* **Audience fit:** very high for Web Summit and Art Week specifically, because attendees form
  LinkedIn and Facebook groups weeks in advance and ask exactly our question.
* **Rules:** usually permissive toward "who is around on the Sunday" posts.
* **Status:** publications 13 and 14 are written for this.

## Tier 3: exists, but not now

### 7. Reddit

* **Audience fit:** the best of any channel on this list, which is why it is worth saying plainly
  that it is unavailable rather than quietly dropping it.
* **Status:** **blocked at the network layer from this machine**, for reading as well as posting, and
  the only account available is a sports betting brand with karma 1 whose last three posts were
  removed by the sitewide spam filter. Ten posts are written and waiting.
* **Risk if forced:** losing the account, which is worth more than the post.

### 8. X

* **Audience fit:** low for a site with no following. X rewards an existing audience, and there is no
  RuinMyTrip account.
* **Status:** two posts written. The only X account here is **BetLegend**, a sports betting brand,
  and posting travel content from it would be both off brand and a risk to an unrelated account.

### 9. TikTok, Instagram, YouTube Shorts

* **Audience fit:** high in principle, and the only channel on this list where reach does not depend
  on an existing audience.
* **Status:** **ten scripts written** in `docs/ACQUISITION_VIDEO.md`. No account exists.
* **Risk:** none to anything else. **Cost:** filming.

### 10. NomadList forum

* **Status: closed.** The forum is archived and read only, and its terms prohibited promotion anyway
  ([nomads.com](https://nomads.com/)). Listed so nobody spends an afternoon rediscovering this.

---

## What this list says, taken together

The channels that fit best are the ones that need a real person with a real account: Facebook groups,
a forum where you answer three questions before you mention yourself, a Discord where you talk for a
week first. **None of them can be automated and none of them should be.** The one channel that needs
no existing audience and no established account is short form video, and that needs somebody to film
ten fifteen second videos.

The honest summary is that acquisition is not blocked on tooling, copy, tracking, landing pages or
analysis. All of that is built and verified. It is blocked on one person spending about two hours
inside communities that require a human being.
