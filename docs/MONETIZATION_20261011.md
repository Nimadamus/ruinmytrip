# RuinMyTrip.com Monetization Research

Research date: 2026-10-11 (all sources below were read on 2026-10-11 unless noted).
Site baseline (measured 2026-10-11): about 35 verified human visitors in 31 days, 1 real member, 0 community reviews, about 15 Google impressions per day.

How to read this document:
* "Official" means the figure came from the program's own page.
* "Third party" means a directory, network listing or blog. These often conflict and go stale.
* "Not verified" means no source confirmed it today. Confirm every rate inside the network dashboard at application time; networks can change rates on short notice (CJ's publisher agreement allows changes on 7 days written notice).
* Nothing was signed up for, no forms were submitted, nobody was contacted.

---

## 1. Key findings

1. **Booking.com no longer runs a direct affiliate programme.** Third party sources report that direct affiliates got termination notices in May 2025 and new applicants since June 2025 go through CJ (or Awin by region), at about 4% of completed stays with session based tracking. Booking.com's own page could not be read today, so this is third party and must be confirmed in CJ.
2. **Experiences pay better than hotels per booking percentage.** Viator pays 8% with a 30 day cookie and has no PayPal minimum (official). GetYourGuide is commonly listed at 8% with a 30 or 31 day cookie (third party). Tiqets 3.5% to 8% and Klook 2% to 5% via Travelpayouts (Travelpayouts listing pages).
3. **Travelpayouts is the best single entry point for a tiny new site.** Its homepage states anyone with a public channel can join, with no minimum traffic mentioned, and it bundles 90 to 100+ brands (Viator, GetYourGuide, Klook, Tiqets, Hostelworld, Kiwi.com and others).
4. **DiscoverCars is unusually generous:** 70% of its profit on rentals, 30% of Full Coverage revenue, 365 day click tracking, only a website URL needed (official).
5. **Display ads are not viable yet.** Ezoic raised its entry bar to 250,000 monthly users in February 2026. Raptive needs 25,000 pageviews per month (official). Mediavine needs $5,000 annual ad revenue; Journey by Mediavine needs 1,000 sessions from Tier 1 countries in 30 days (official). AdSense has no stated traffic minimum but requires high quality original content (official). At 35 visitors a month, ads would earn cents and would hurt the look of a trust brand.
6. **The legal line is clear and enforced.** The FTC Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (16 CFR Part 465) took effect 21 October 2024. The FTC sent warning letters to ten companies on 22 December 2025 citing penalties up to $53,088 per violation. The EU blacklist (UCPD Annex I points 23b and 23c, added by the Omnibus Directive) bans fake reviews and claiming reviews are genuine without reasonable verification steps, and requires saying whether and how reviews are checked.
7. **Realistic revenue at today's traffic is zero.** Even at 1,000 monthly visitors, the estimate is roughly $3 to $90 a month (midpoint around $15), and payout minimums may delay any cash. Monetization matters only after traffic grows; the first job is content and reviews.

## 3. Recommended placements that fit the product

RuinMyTrip's brand is honest, sometimes negative reviews. The one placement mistake that would destroy it is sending someone to book the very place travelers warned them about, or making a "ruined" venue look good because it pays. Every module below is built around that risk.

### 3.1 Place pages (hotels, restaurants, attractions)

* **"Stay nearby" module**, placed below the review list and below the community rating, inside a visibly separate box with a light background and a header such as "Places to stay near [Place]".
  * Show a neutral map or list of nearby accommodation (Booking.com via CJ, Expedia/Hotels.com, Agoda for Asia, Hostelworld for hostel heavy cities). A map widget style aggregator (for example Stay22, which reports +22% average RPM uplift for publishers, vendor claim, not verified) is an option because it shows many options without endorsing one.
  * On a hotel's own place page, never show a "Book this hotel" button if its community rating is poor. Show "Alternatives nearby" instead. This turns the honest review into the reason to click, which is both more trustworthy and likely to convert better (estimate).
* **"Book this experience" module** on attraction pages (museums, tours, landmarks) via Viator, GetYourGuide, Tiqets or Klook, showing skip the line tickets or tours that include the attraction. Same separation rules.
* Each module carries a short disclosure line directly above the links (see 4.3).

### 3.2 City pages

* "Where to stay in [City]" neighborhood box, drawing on the site's own honest notes about areas, with accommodation links underneath.
* "Top bookable experiences in [City]" carousel, ordered by partner rating or price, never by commission.

### 3.3 Trip planning and the travel map tool

* After a user colors their travel map or saves a planned trip, a "Before you go" checklist: eSIM (Airalo, Holafly), travel insurance (SafetyWing, Heymondo, World Nomads), car rental (DiscoverCars), flights (Skyscanner, Kiwi.com). This is the highest intent moment on the site and does not touch reviews at all.
* Insurance needs extra care: show it as information plus a link, not as advice. Rules on insurance promotion vary by country (not researched in depth; not verified).

### 3.4 Travel buddy matching

* No affiliate links inside buddy messages or profiles. Optionally a "plan the trip together" page with the same "Before you go" checklist.

### 3.5 Premium features (later, only once there are real users)

* Membership extras that do not touch ratings: offline city guides, trip export, ad free view, advanced map styles.
* Business "claim this listing" with the right to respond publicly to reviews, add photos and hours. Never the right to remove, hide, reorder or rate reviews.

## 4. Trust rules (proposed house policy)

### 4.1 What the big platforms do

* **Tripadvisor:** paid hotel and restaurant Sponsored Placements are pay per click, carry a grey "Sponsored" label, and Tripadvisor stated they "would not affect organic rank order" and that paid reviews break its review bias rules. Critics note sponsored listings can still sit above higher rated places. Sources are 2017 to 2018 coverage (triptease.com/blog/tripadvisor-ads/; arival.travel coverage; travelweekly.com.au). Current 2026 policy not verified.
* **Yelp:** states advertising does not influence its recommendation software and that advertisers and non advertisers are treated the same; a class action alleging otherwise was dismissed and the dismissal affirmed by the Ninth Circuit (conductatlas.com Yelp terms provision; fsrmagazine.com; bcbusiness.ca). Yelp's current ad label wording not verified.
* **Trustpilot:** businesses may invite reviews but must not offer incentives, try to influence content, or invite only happy customers; incentivized reviews are removed and the business faces action (corporate.trustpilot.com/legal/for-everyone/action-we-take).
* **Google Maps:** bans incentives for reviews; secondary sources report that from February 2026 it explicitly covers benefits offered to remove a negative review (makeinfluence.com; pinmeto.com). Google's own current text not read today.

Common pattern: money can buy visibility in clearly labeled ad slots, never reviews, never ratings, never organic ranking.

### 4.2 RuinMyTrip rules

1. **Never inside a review.** No affiliate link, ad or sponsored element is ever rendered inside, between or styled like a user review.
2. **Never tied to ratings.** Ratings, review order and "worst of" or "best of" rankings are computed only from reviews. No code path reads commission, partner status or advertiser status when sorting or rating.
3. **No paid placement in rankings.** If sponsored listings are ever sold, they appear only in a separate labeled slot ("Sponsored") and never inside ranked lists.
4. **Businesses cannot buy review changes.** No removal, hiding, delay or reordering of reviews for payment. If any delay for moderation exists, it applies equally to positive and negative reviews (FTC guidance).
5. **No incentives conditioned on sentiment.** If reviews are ever rewarded (points, badges), the reward is the same for any honest review, positive or negative, and the incentive is disclosed.
6. **Default sort is not star rating descending.** Default to most recent or most helpful (FTC guidance says high star first default sorting can be deceptive).
7. **Every paid link uses rel="sponsored"** (Google: "Mark links that are advertisements or paid placements... with the sponsored value").
8. **Public review authenticity statement** explaining whether and how reviews are checked (EU requirement, see 5.3).
9. **A public "How we make money" page**, linked from the footer and from every disclosure line.

### 4.3 Disclosure wording

Next to each module (not only in the footer):

> We earn a commission if you book through these links. It never changes our ratings or which reviews you see. [How we make money]

Short form for small widgets: "Paid links. We earn a commission. Ratings are never affected."

FTC basis: the FTC FAQ says "the closer the disclosure is to your recommendation, the better", gives "I get commissions for purchases made through links in this post" as an example, says "paid link" next to an affiliate link should be adequate, says "affiliate link" alone and a "buy now" button may not be enough, and says a single home page disclosure or a link labeled "DISCLOSURE" is not sufficient.

## 5. Legal disclosure requirements

### 5.1 United States: FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255, revised 2023)

* The Guides were revised in 2023 and include a definition of "clearly and conspicuously" (FTC FAQ page notes the revision; the 2023 adoption date of 29 June 2023 is from prior knowledge and was not re-read today).
* Affiliate relationships must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously, close to the recommendation; a single home page disclosure is not enough.
* Incentivized reviews must be disclosed; if they inflate a summary rating, disclose next to the summary rating; incentives cannot be conditioned on positive reviews.
* Review gating (asking only happy customers) is misleading if it substantially skews favorability; delaying only negative reviews and asking unhappy customers to delete reviews are problematic.
* Source: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking (read 2026-10-11).

### 5.2 United States: FTC Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (16 CFR Part 465)

* Announced 14 August 2024, effective 21 October 2024.
* Bans fake reviews (including AI generated and from people with no real experience), buying or selling them, conditional incentives for reviews of a particular sentiment (positive or negative), undisclosed insider reviews, review suppression, and fake indicators of social media influence.
* Enforcement: warning letters to ten companies on 22 December 2025, citing civil penalties up to $53,088 per violation. The 2026 inflation adjusted figure is not verified. One less established source claims five settlements totaling $27.4 million in April 2026; not verified against an FTC release.
* Sources: https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2024/09/alerts-practices-cldr-ftc-finalizes-rule-on-consumer-reviews ; https://alston.com/en/insights/publications/2024/10/ftc-issues-final-rule-on-fake-reviews-testimonials ; https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/blogs/consumer-products-and-retail-navigator/2026/01/ftc-warning-letters-over-consumer-review-rule ; https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2025/12/ftc-signals-heightened-enforcement-of-new ; https://beancount.io/blog/2026/07/16/ftc-fake-reviews-crackdown-consumer-review-rule-guide (all read 2026-10-11).

### 5.3 European Union: Omnibus Directive (EU 2019/2161) and UCPD

* Applies from 28 May 2022.
* Traders that provide access to consumer reviews must state whether and how they ensure reviews come from consumers who actually used or bought the product.
* Blacklisted in all circumstances (UCPD Annex I): point 23b, claiming reviews come from real users without reasonable and proportionate steps to check; point 23c, submitting or commissioning fake reviews or endorsements, or misrepresenting reviews (for example showing only positive reviews).
* RuinMyTrip implication: publish a short authenticity statement (for example "Reviews come from registered members. We do not verify bookings. We remove reviews we find to be fake, paid or from the business itself.") and never claim "verified traveler" unless a real check exists.
* Sources: https://www.ey.com/en_pl/insights/law/omnibus-directive ; https://mondaq.com/dodd-frank-consumer-protection-act/1189542/the-omnibus-directive-consumer-reviews ; https://www.makeinfluence.com/en/academy/fake-and-manipulated-reviews-what-the-eu-omnibus-directive-bans (read 2026-10-11). Exact Annex I wording should be checked on EUR-Lex (not read today).

### 5.4 European Union: Digital Services Act

* Article 26 (labeling ads shown on online platforms, saying who paid) sits in Section 3, and Article 19 exempts micro and small enterprises from Section 3, except Article 24(3). RuinMyTrip likely qualifies as micro, so Article 26 likely does not bind it today; labeling ads anyway costs nothing and matches the trust rules.
* Basic hosting duties (notice and action for illegal content, terms of service transparency) still apply to all hosting services. Not researched in depth.
* Sources: https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/dsa/dsa-regulation/article-19/ ; https://www.heuking.de/en/news-events/newsletter-articles/detail/online-marketing-pursuant-to-the-digital-services-act.html (read 2026-10-11).

### 5.5 Google Search

* Paid and affiliate links: rel="sponsored" preferred, rel="nofollow" acceptable. https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/qualify-outbound-links (read 2026-10-11).

Not legal advice. The UK (DMCC Act 2024 fake review rules) and other jurisdictions were not researched.

Published EPC figures (for example $112.50 for Booking.com, $97.78 for Hotels.com on authorityhacker.com) are undated, from one blog, and EPC definitions vary by network, so they are **not verified** and are not used below. ## 7. Staged plan

### Now (about 35 visitors a month)

* Do not place affiliate modules or ads yet. They earn nothing, and on a site with zero community reviews they make it look like an affiliate farm.
* Write and publish the trust pieces first: "How we make money" page, review authenticity statement, review policy (no incentives tied to sentiment, equal moderation, no removal for payment).
* Build the module templates (Stay nearby, Book this experience, Before you go) behind a feature flag, with rel="sponsored" and disclosure lines baked in, and with code that structurally cannot read partner data when computing ratings or sort order.
* Focus all effort on traffic and the first real reviews.

### At 1,000 monthly visitors

* Apply to Travelpayouts (one account, many brands, no stated minimum) and Viator direct. Apply to DiscoverCars and SafetyWing direct.
* Turn on the "Before you go" checklist on the travel map and trip planning flow first (furthest from reviews), then "Book this experience" on attraction pages.
* Track clicks per page type in your own analytics to replace the assumptions in section 6 with real numbers.
* No display ads.

### At 10,000 monthly visitors

* Apply to CJ for Booking.com and to the Expedia Group Creator Program; add Agoda for Asian cities and Hostelworld for backpacker cities. Turn on "Stay nearby" on place and city pages, with "Alternatives nearby" on poorly rated hotels.
* Consider Journey by Mediavine if Tier 1 sessions exceed 1,000 per 30 days, but only below the fold and never between reviews. Test whether ads reduce review submissions before keeping them.
* Ask partners for higher tiers only once volume justifies it.

### At 100,000 monthly visitors

* Apply to Raptive (25,000 PV minimum) or Mediavine (once $5,000 annual ad revenue is reachable) and compare against affiliate only layouts.
* Introduce premium membership extras and business "claim your listing" tools that never touch ratings or review visibility.
* If selling sponsored placements, use a single labeled "Sponsored" slot outside ranked lists, publish the policy, and log every sponsored placement so it can be audited.
* Get a formal legal review of disclosure and review practices for the main traffic countries (US, EU, UK).
