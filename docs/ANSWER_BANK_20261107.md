## 1. "My flight from the EU was delayed/cancelled. What am I owed?"
> If it left from the EU (or flew into the EU on an EU airline) you are likely covered by EU261. For a
> cancellation, or arriving 3+ hours late, it is EUR 250 up to 1,500 km, EUR 400 for 1,500–3,500 km
> (and longer flights within the EU), and EUR 600 over 3,500 km, unless the cause was "extraordinary"
> (weather, air traffic control, security). Most technical faults and the airline's own staff strikes
> do not count as extraordinary. Claim from the airline first; if nothing after 2 months, go to the
> national enforcement body in the country where it happened. Doing it yourself is free.
> Disclosure: I run RuinMyTrip; the full rundown with the official links is here: {claims link}

## 2. "US airline cancelled and only offered a voucher"
> Under DOT's refund rule (14 CFR 260) you are owed cash if the airline cancels or makes a significant
> change you do not accept: 3+ hours late domestic, 6+ international, a different airport, extra
> connections or a downgrade. They cannot treat you as accepting a voucher unless you agree. The refund
> is due within 7 business days on a credit card, 20 days otherwise. There is no US cash compensation
> for delays. (One exception right now: a flight that is only renumbered is not being enforced.)
> Disclosure: I run RuinMyTrip; sources here: {claims link}

## 3. "Airline lost/damaged my bag, how much can I get?"
> On international flights the Montreal Convention cap is 1,519 SDR per passenger (it went up from
> 1,288 in December 2024, and lots of pages still quote the old number). The deadlines are what catch
> people: complain in writing within 7 days for damage and within 21 days of getting a delayed bag
> back. On US flights you also get the checked bag fee back if it was 12+ hours late domestic, but
> only if you filed a Mishandled Baggage Report, so do it at the airport.
> Disclosure: I run RuinMyTrip; sources here: {claims link}

## 4. "Is this gem shop / tuk-tuk thing in Bangkok a scam?"
> Canada's travel advice describes it almost word for word: merchants sell lower-quality gems at
> inflated prices with promises you can resell them at a profit, and the guarantees are not honoured.
> Don't buy gems as an investment. The other ones official advice warns about: bars that charge
> exorbitant bills (agree prices first, don't run a tab), and rental shops that "find" damage and keep
> your passport. Never leave your passport as a deposit.
> Disclosure: I run RuinMyTrip; the list with each government source: {scams-bangkok-thailand link}

## 5. "Pickpockets / airport taxis in Rome, Paris, Barcelona?"
> UK and Canadian advice both describe the distraction method: someone spills a drink on you, starts a
> long conversation or keeps offering help while a partner takes your things, mostly on public
> transport and at the big sights. At Fiumicino the airport operator says any car offering rides in
> arrivals may not be licensed: use the white taxis from the rank. (Swap in the city you are asked about.)
> Disclosure: I run RuinMyTrip; sourced list: {scams-rome-italy / scams-paris-france / scams-barcelona-spain link}

## 6. "How much is the Barcelona tourist tax for a family / a long stay?"
> From 1 April 2026 it is the Catalan rate plus a 5 euro city surcharge, per person per night: 8.40 in a
> four star, 12 in a five star, 9.50 in a tourist apartment. Two rules people miss: under-17s pay
> nothing, and only the first 7 nights per person are taxed. So 10 nights for two adults in a four
> star is 117.60, not 168. The surcharge goes to 6 euros from April 2027.
> Disclosure: I run RuinMyTrip; there is a calculator here: {tax-calculator link}

## 7. "Lisbon tourist tax?"
> 4 euros per guest per night, at most 7 nights per stay, and under-13s are exempt (Lisbon's own
> regulation). Usually paid at the property.
> Disclosure: I run RuinMyTrip; calculator: {tax-calculator link}

## 8. "Is the Venice day-tripper fee on right now?"
> Not until next year: the 2026 fee days ended on 26 July. The council has approved 72 fee days for
> 2027 starting Good Friday, 26 March; the amounts and booking rules are still to be announced on
> cda.ve.it. Overnight guests have been exempt in past years, but check the 2027 rules when they are out.
> (No link needed; this answer stands alone.)

## 9. "Edinburgh Castle tickets for a family?"
> There is no family ticket on the castle's own page. Adults are £23.50 online and £26 at the gate,
> children 7–15 £11.50 online and £13 at the gate, under-7s free but still added to the booking. Two
> adults and two kids: £70 online, £78 at the gate. Book ahead; it sells out and the gate can't help.
> (No link needed.)

## 10. "Sagrada Familia, what if we are late for our slot?"
> You won't get in: the official FAQ says tickets are only valid for the time chosen and late arrivals
> can't enter. Under-11s are free. Book on sagradafamilia.org, not a reseller.
> (No link needed.)
